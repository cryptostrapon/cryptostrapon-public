# Before you set up a hardware wallet

A short checklist written after the 2026 hardware implant incident. Criminals tampered with genuine Ledger devices at the end of the distribution chain. They added a hidden part that captured the recovery phrase during setup, and the devices then reached buyers through third-party sellers.

Ledger has confirmed cases in Southeast Asia and Berlin and is investigating reports of two units in Dubai. It reports no flaw in its secure chip or operating system and no confirmed case involving a device bought on ledger.com ([Ledger update, 18 November 2026](https://support.ledger.com/article/Update-on-the-Hardware-Implant-Incident-Investigation)).

The lesson applies to every brand: a hardware wallet is only as safe as the path it took to reach you.

Español: [hardware-wallet-setup.es.md](hardware-wallet-setup.es.md)

## 1. Where to buy

- [ ] Buy from the manufacturer's own store. Type the address yourself instead of following an ad, a search result or a message
- [ ] If you buy from a shop, check that the manufacturer lists it as an official reseller, and keep the receipt
- [ ] Skip second-hand devices, marketplace listings and bundles that are cheaper than the official price

## 2. Before you set it up

- [ ] The device creates a new recovery phrase itself. If a phrase came printed in the box, on a card or in a message, do not use that device
- [ ] Nobody needs your recovery phrase: not support, not a shop, not an app. Ledger says it will never ask for the 24 words, including Ledger Support
- [ ] Do not open the case to look for an implant. Contact the manufacturer's support instead

## 3. If you doubt a device

- [ ] Stop using it. Do not set up or restore a wallet on it
- [ ] Bought a Ledger from a third party in Southeast Asia, in Berlin since January 2026, or in Dubai? Contact Ledger Support through ledger.com
- [ ] For a limited time, Ledger Support can arrange a physical inspection, including X-ray analysis, of devices that raise concerns
- [ ] If funds were lost, file a report with your local authorities

## 4. Moving funds to safety

- [ ] Get a new device from the official store
- [ ] Create a brand new recovery phrase on it. Do not reuse the old phrase or passphrase: Ledger warns that a tampered device can capture both
- [ ] Send a small test amount first, check the receiving address on the device screen, then move the rest

## 5. For larger amounts

- [ ] Consider a multisig wallet, for example 2 of 3, with signers from different manufacturers bought through separate channels. One compromised device then cannot move funds alone
- [ ] Keep recovery phrases offline: never photographed, typed into a computer or phone, or stored in the cloud
